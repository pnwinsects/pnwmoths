## Identification

##### Adults

*Papaipema unimoda* is a variable medium size (FW length 13–17 mm) borer moth that has only been found in western Montana in our region during September. The forewing is typical for the genus, relatively broad distally with pointed apex and smoothly convex outer margin. The ground color is typically dark bronze, but lighter golden or yellow-orange forms appear commonly in the Montana population. Dark bronze specimens are uniform except for purplish gray between the postmedial and subterminal lines, while the more yellow forms tend to be darker brownish orange on the wing base and have only slightly grayer subterminal area. The basal and antemedial lines are absent or faint. The medial line is darker than the ground, evenly excurved with apex just posterior to the reniform spot. The postmedial line is double, fairly straight, slightly scalloped and excurved lateral to the reniform spot and then slightly concave to the trailing margin. The subterminal line is slightly irregular and mildly scalloped, mostly evident as pale scales against the dark distal wing color.  The large orbicular, suborbicular, and reniform spots are also variable in this species, typically dark brownish orange but occasionally white broken into smaller units by netlike dark lines. The hindwing is dark gray in dark bronze specimens and yellowish with gray suffusion and faint discal spot and postmedial line in lighter ones. The head, thorax, and abdomen match the forewing ground color. The male antenna is serrate but relatively narrow.

This species is similar to our other *Papaipema* species, but only *Papaipema insulidens* and *Papaipema limata* are known to occur close to western Montana. Most *P*. *unimoda* are darker than either of these species, especially *P*. *limata*. The morph of *P*. *unimoda* with dark-filled spots cannot be confused with any other moth in our region. Specimens of *P*. *unimoda* with white-filled spots resemble *P*. *insulidens* but tend to be either darker brownish bronze or yellower and less mottled; however, some specimens cannot be reliably distinguished without dissection or association with its food plant. *Hydroeciodes serrata* is a similar but not-closely related moth in the tribe Eriopygini that could be confused with the white-spotted form of *P*. *unimoda*. Males of *H*. *serrata* have a much wider biserrate antenna and both sexes have tiny hairs on the eyes that are lacking in *Papaipema*. Other differences between these moths are discussed under *H*. *serrata*.

## Habitat

The only site for this moth in the Pacific Northwest is riparian forest along the Bitterroot River.

## Distribution

### Pacific
 Northwest

*Papaipema unimoda* is at the most northwesterly part of its range in western Montana. It has only been found at one locality in the Bitterroot Valley south of Missoula in our region. It is likely to be more widespread in western Montana and could potentially occur in eastern Idaho.

### Global

This moth is widespread in eastern and central North America. It occurs from Alberta and Colorado in the west and from North Carolina to southern Canada in the East.

## Life
History

##### Larvae

The larvae bore in the roots and stems of several plants, most commonly meadow rue (*Thalictrum* (Ranunculaceae)). Other recorded hosts include jacob's ladder (*Polemonium* (Polymoniaceae)) and cone flowers (*Rudbeckia* (Asteraceae)).

##### Adults

Adults typically fly during the fall, most commonly in September. The few PNW records are from early and mid September. They are nocturnal and come to lights.

## Economic Importance

None.
